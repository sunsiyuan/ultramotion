# Pixel Art

Pixel Art (像素 8-bit) — makes a VIDEO (mp4 with sound) in low-resolution pixel art: a small grid of pixels drawn by hand-like code, blown up with hard edges, every colour snapped to a small palette, moving on a stepped clock like an old console game, with chiptune music and console sound effects. Draws scenes (cities, overworld maps, rooms, objects, icons), bitmap and pixelated type, RPG text boxes, level-up screens, and turns the user's own photo or footage into pixel art. In the manner of NES and Game Boy screens, the Celeste trailers, waneella's pixel loops and Paul Robertson's animation.

`pixel.js` gives you a small canvas — the grid, e.g. 320×180 — to draw on each frame; it snaps every pixel to your palette and blows the grid up to the video with hard edges, on the clock you choose. `chip.py` plays chiptune and console sound effects; `render.py` renders the page to mp4 with sound. Open any file in the template folders (`templates/`) through a local server (`python3 -m http.server`) and it plays; `render.py` loads them directly.

## Workflow

1. Copy `lib/pixel/pixel.js` into your project; work on the copies.
2. Read the content: what the piece is for, where it plays, who watches it (*What to make*). Pick the grid, the palette and the clock from that (*Grid, palette, clock*).
3. Put everything that a user would change — words, colours, the photo, the route, the price — in one block at the top of the page.
4. Draw the picture as a function of time on the grid; look at stills (`--preview --size 0` shows frames at full size).
5. Put the sounds on the picture's events and write the music (*Sound*).
6. `python3 $SKILL/render.py page.html --preview --at 1,3.5,6 --size 0` · `python3 $SKILL/render.py page.html --out video.mp4` · `--audio --out x.wav` renders only the sound.

## What to make

Pixel art here is a way of showing the content, not the subject. The copy says what the stream, the shop, the trip or the birthday would say. Games lend the piece its verbs — a title dropping in, a text box typing, a level-up, a flag raised, a map with a path, a countdown, an iris closing — use the ones the content can carry.

- **Stream and channel screens** — starting soon, be right back, an intro, an outro, a schedule: a looping scene with the title, the handle and a countdown.
- **Posters and screens for a place or an event** — a café's menu screen, a new drink or dish, an arcade bar night, a game jam, a shop opening: one object drawn well, the name, the price, the date; often a loop.
- **Greetings with the user's own photo** — a birthday, an anniversary, a pet, a kid, a team photo: the photo becomes pixel art inside a game screen (a battle screen, a character card, a level-up).
- **Maps and levels that explain** — a trip itinerary, a route, a roadmap, an onboarding, a course, steps of a process: an overworld map with a path and stops, or one level per step; real places are placed by their coordinates.
- **Launches and announcements** — an indie game, an app feature, a release date: a title screen with *press start*, a stat sheet, an unlock.
- **Music and lyrics** — a chiptune single, a lyric card: a looping scene with words in text boxes.

What the picture can be made of:
- **Drawn scenes** — skylines as rectangles with windows, terrain tiles, rooms, skies in dithered bands, weather; generated by code from a seed, so they are big and consistent.
- **Objects and icons** — a cup, a cake, a coin, a heart, a key, a flag, a leaf, a car icon: simple silhouettes drawn with shapes or sprite rows, lit from one side.
- **The user's photo or footage** — `Pixel.photo()` turns it into pixel art in the palette; `Pixel.footage()` does it to every frame of a video.
- **Type** — bitmap Latin fonts at 1×–4×, Chinese from installed fonts rasterised with hard edges, boxes and bars like a game's interface.
- **People** — `figure()` builds a person from parts posed by their joints, shaded in three tones with a dark line where limbs overlap, in the manner of 16-bit sports and fighting games: boxers, sprinters, a crowd of runners, a trader, a player. One pose is a handful of points, so a move is a few poses and `tween` fills the frames between. A look is colours: skin, shirt, shorts or trousers, shoes, hair, gloves, a headband.

**Draw only what comes out well.** People come from `figure()`; animals, faces close up, hands and machines drawn by hand at more than ~12 px tall come out wrong: take them from a photo, keep them as tiny silhouettes, or leave them out and let objects, places and type carry it.

## How much

Choose the energy from where it plays.

- **A calm loop** — a menu screen, a waiting screen, a lo-fi music loop, a greeting card: almost everything holds; a few small things move on a slow clock (steam, leaves, a blinking window, a cursor); a build-in at the start, a build-out at the end so it loops.
- **A game's rhythm** — a map explainer, a birthday level-up, a launch: one event after another, each with its sound — typed lines, a flag, a counter, a fanfare — and holds to read between them.
- **Arcade** — a stream intro, a game-night poster, a hype reel: the scene scrolls, titles drop and shake, counters tick, music with drums under everything, a hard ending (iris, wipe).

## How it moves

- **On a stepped clock.** `fps` is how often the picture changes: 8–12 for a hand-animated, console feel; 15 for scrolling scenes; 30 only for things that must glide. Slow details step slower still — `Pixel.step(t, 4)`, `Pixel.anim(frames, t, 6)`, `Pixel.blink(t, 2)`.
- **In whole pixels.** Everything lands on the grid: positions are rounded, scrolls are `Math.floor(t * speed)`, parallax layers move at different whole-pixel speeds.
- **With game verbs.** Drop in with gravity and a small bounce, `hop`, `shake` on a landing, slide in by blocks, type text at a fixed rate, raise a flag in three steps, fill a bar in segments, count in whole numbers.
- **Transitions the console way.** `wipe` (tiles, blinds, bars, dissolve, a direction), `iris` closing on the subject, `mosaic` blocks growing or resolving, `fade` in three or four hard steps — not a smooth cross-fade.
- **Holds.** Let each title, line and number sit long enough to read; text boxes wait with a blinking arrow.
- **People in key poses.** A move is a few poses held for a frame or two each, at 8–15 fps: a guard that bobs between two; a punch out in two frames and back in one; a hit that snaps the head back and drops the hands; a fall in three; a run in six (contact, pass, push off, then the other side). Build the poses once, render each with `figure()` before the first frame, and pick one per frame.
- **Loops.** For screens that repeat, make every moving thing periodic in the loop length (steam, leaves, windows), and match the first and last frames.

## Grid, palette, clock

- **Grid** — the pixels you draw on. It must divide the video exactly: 1920×1080 → 320×180 (×6), 240×135 (×8), 480×270 (×4); 1080×1920 → 180×320 (×6), 135×240 (×8); 1080×1080 → 180×180, 135×135. Fewer pixels = chunkier and bolder (posters, greetings); more = room for detail and small type (maps, interfaces).
- **Palette** — every pixel is snapped to it. Built in: `gameboy` and `pocket` (4 tones), `cga`, `pico8`, `sweetie16`, `c64`, `nes`. Or write your own — 4 tones for a handheld feel, 8–16 for a scene — chosen for the content: the shop's colours, a dusk, a season. `dither` (0..1) turns smooth fills into patterns when snapping; leave it at 0 when you draw flat.
- **Screen** — `look: { scan }` darkens a line under each pixel row (a TV), `look: { grid }` leaves gaps between pixels (a handheld LCD); 0 for a clean modern pixel look.

## The page

```html
<canvas></canvas>
<script src="pixel.js"></script>
<script>
/* ===== change these ===== */
const CONTENT = { title: 'STARTING SOON', photo: 'media/me.jpg' };
const COLORS = { ink: '#0d0b1e', sky: '#2d2556', gold: '#ffd27a', cream: '#fff4d6' };
/* ======================== */
const P = Pixel;
window.__scene = P.scene(async () => {
  await P.fonts(['400 12px "Hiragino Sans GB"']);
  const img = await P.image(CONTENT.photo);
  const pic = P.photo(img, { w: 96, h: 96, palette: Object.values(COLORS), dither: 'ordered' });
  return {
    width: 1920, height: 1080, grid: [320, 180], duration: 12, fps: 12,
    palette: Object.values(COLORS), look: { scan: .15 },
    draw(g, t) {                                         // g: the grid's 2D context; t is already stepped
      P.gradient(g, 0, 0, 320, 120, [COLORS.sky, COLORS.ink]);
      g.drawImage(pic, 20, 40);
      P.text(g, CONTENT.title, 160, 20, { font: '5x7', scale: 3, color: COLORS.cream, outline: COLORS.ink, align: 'center' });
    },
    sounds: [{ t: 1.0, kind: 'land' }, ...P.typeSounds('hello', 2, 20)],
    music: { chip: [P.chip.note(0, 60, .4, 'pulse'), ...P.chip.drums(0, 'k.h.s.h.', 120, 4)] },
  };
});
</script>
```

`draw` must depend only on `t`. Use `P.rng(seed)` and `P.hash(…)` for anything random.

**Drawing** (whole pixels): `rect(g, x, y, w, h, col)` · `px` · `line` · `circle(g, cx, cy, r, col, solid)` · `shape(g, col, c => { c.beginPath(); c.ellipse(…); c.fill(); })` — any canvas path, filled with hard edges · `dither(g, x, y, w, h, a, b, level)` · `gradient(g, x, y, w, h, [cols], 'v'|'h')` — bands with dithered steps.
**Sprites**: `sprite(['..aa..', '.abba.'], { a: '#hex', b: '#hex' })` (`.` is clear; an array of row-arrays gives frames) · `put(g, spr, x, y, { flip, scale, anchor: 'tl'|'c'|'bc' })` · `anim(frames, t, fps)` · `outline(spr, col)` · `colorize(spr, col)`.
**Type**: `text(g, str, x, y, { font, scale, color, align, valign, outline, shadow: { color, dx, dy }, spacing, lead })` returns `{ w, h }`; `y` is the top. Fonts: `'5x7'` (ASCII and · • ♥ ★ → ← … × °), `'3x5'` (capitals, digits, marks), or an installed font as a CSS string — for Chinese `'400 12px "Hiragino Sans GB"'` or `"PingFang SC"` at 12–16 px for lines, `'900 24px "PingFang SC"'` and up for titles. `measure`, `typed(str, t, t0, cps)`, `textbox(g, { x, y, w, h, text, t, t0, cps, font, color, bg, border, arrow })`.
**People**: `figure(pose, look, { scale, w, h, light, ink })` → an outlined canvas, facing right (`put(…, { flip: true })` for left). `pose`: `head`, `neck`, `hip`, and `far` / `near` (the side away from / toward the camera) each `{ sh, el, ha, kn, ft }` — shoulder, elbow, hand, knee, foot — as `[x, y]` in a 40×40 box with the feet near the bottom; optional `tilt` (head, radians) and `gaze` (`'up'`, `'down'`, `'shut'`). `look`: `skin`, `top`, `bottom`, `shoes`, `hair` (a colour, or `[light, mid, dark]`), `gloves`, `band`, `belt`, `short: true` for shorts, `sleeves: true` when the top covers the arms. `scale: 2` draws the same pose twice as big at the same pixel size, with more detail. `tween(poseA, poseB, u)` → the pose between. `figureColors(look)` → the colours it uses — add them to the scene's `palette`.
**Photos and footage**: `photo(img, { w, h, palette, dither: 'ordered'|'fs'|0, strength, contrast, saturation, brightness, fx, fy, zoom })` → a canvas; `palette` can be a number (that many colours taken from the photo). Crop close on the subject and raise the contrast for small grids and 4-tone palettes. `footage('clip.mp4')` → `.draw(g, t, photoOptions)` (render.py splits the video into frames; use `ordered`, not `fs`, on video). `paletteFrom(img, k)`.
**Effects on the grid** (call at the end of `draw`): `mosaic(g, n, box)` · `fade(g, u, col, steps)` · `iris(g, cx, cy, r, col)` · `wipe(g, 'tiles'|'blinds'|'bars'|'dissolve'|'left'|'right'|'up'|'down', u, col, { size, box })`.
**Timing**: `step(t, fps)` · `blink(t, rate, duty)` · `hop(t, t0, dur, h)` · `glide(t, t0, t1, a, b, ease)` · `count(t, t0, dur, from, to)` · `shake(t, t0, d, amt)` · `seg lerp io oc sm cl`.

## Sound

Console sound effects, from `chip.py`, each on the frame its event happens: `coin` (got it, a stop reached, a price), `jump`, `land` (a title lands), `select` (a menu line, a letter), `confirm` / `cancel`, `text` (one per typed character — `P.typeSounds(str, t0, cps, { every, v })` makes them), `step` (walking, driving), `count` / `go` (a countdown), `powerup` (a bar filling), `levelup` (a fanfare), `oneup`, `hurt`, `explode`, `warp`, `door`, `pause`. `pitch` shifts one in semitones. `sound.py` kinds (`whoosh`, `thud`, `tick`, `chime` …) mix in too.

Music is written as notes on the console's four channels — `music: { chip: [...] }`:
- `pulse`, `pulse2` — square waves; `duty` .5 round, .25 bright, .125 thin. The tune on one, broken chords or a harmony on the other, quieter.
- `tri` — the bass, or a soft low tune.
- `noise` — drums: `P.chip.drums(t0, 'k.h.s.h.', bpm, bars)` (k kick, s snare, h hat, o open hat).
- `P.chip.arp(t, [60, 64, 67], dur)` sounds a chord as a fast arpeggio; `vib` and `slide` on long notes.

Fit it to the piece: a calm loop or a greeting — two or three voices, slow, no drums or only a soft hat, loop length in whole bars; a map or a level — a walking tune with a triangle bass; arcade — drums, arpeggios, a faster tune. Keep the tune in the middle (MIDI 60–79), the bass on the triangle; leave a rest where a fanfare or a big sound effect plays. `music: { notes: [...] }` plays sampled instruments through `sound.py` instead or as well (a music box, a kalimba) when the piece wants something softer than chip.

## Check your output

- **blurry or uneven pixels** — the grid doesn't divide the video, something is drawn at fractional positions, or a photo was scaled onto the grid with smoothing on: fix the grid, round, draw photos through `photo()`.
- **stray colours, soft edges on shapes** — draw shapes with `shape()` or the pixel helpers instead of plain canvas paths; keep the palette small.
- **it moves like a website** — smooth easing and 30 fps glides everywhere: step the clock, move in whole pixels, use the game verbs.
- **it never stops moving, or never moves** — check *How much* against where it plays.
- **a figure that looks wrong** — a hand-drawn animal or machine: use the photo, shrink it to a silhouette, or remove it. A person: build them with `figure()`; joints at full length (forearm and shin about as long as upper arm and thigh), both feet on the ground line unless running or falling.
- **skin gone orange or green** — the figure's colours were snapped to the palette: add `figureColors(look)` to it.
- **text you can't read** — Chinese below 12 px, Latin below the 5×7 font, or no outline on a busy background.
- **the photo is mush** — crop closer on the subject, raise contrast, give it more pixels or more colours.
- **sounds miss the picture** — build every sound from the same times the picture uses, never typed twice.
- **the copy talks about pixels or retro games** — write what the stream, the shop, the trip, the owner would say.
